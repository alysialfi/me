# Five years in tech, one lesson: build for the business

I believe technology exists to help a business align with its markets and customers. In my 5+ years of working in tech across several industries, I have tried my best to integrate technology with business needs.

The first job I landed was supposed to be as a mobile developer. However, the company needed more people to work on databases and web development, so I learned to query and to code in .NET and JS to fit the need.

A year later, I moved to my current company as a front-end developer. I decided to dive deeper into this field when my interest was sparked by React.js, a library that enables JS developers to build interactive, reactive web apps with a declarative rather than imperative approach, which I believe leads to a better developer experience.

In my first year, the team used jQuery as our JS library. In my second year, we decided to pick up a newer tech stack. The discussion was long, since we had several candidates: React.js, Vue.js, Svelte, and Alpine. The biggest determinant was our backend and third-party stack. We rely heavily on PHP, and WHMCS (Web Host Manager Complete Solution), our all-in-one client management, billing, and automation system, is also built on PHP. Advice and experience from senior colleagues with certain stacks also mattered. We ended up choosing Vue.js. It was well known for its integration with Laravel back then, and my seniors already worked with it a lot, so we believed the transition would be easier.

To this day we still use that stack, and we have built the system without big issues. Services that are not coupled to specific integrations can use a different stack, and we are open to that flexibility. The stack matters less than the purpose. Whether it is Vue.js, React.js, or something else, technology should help the business do what it needs to do. Delivering that is always my task as part of the tech team.
